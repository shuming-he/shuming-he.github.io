Same bag. Two prices.

Luxury list prices compared across the border · United States vs Canada

Abstract

Is the same luxury bag or watch cheaper in Canada or in the United States once the exchange rate and sales tax are accounted for? I collected official list prices for matched products from each brand's US and Canadian websites and tested the gap with a paired t-test on log price ratios.

View

Real prices. Every price is an official list price read from the brand's own US and Canadian websites, and every product links to both source pages. This is a snapshot, not a live feed: brands change prices, so check the source before you buy.

Method

From product pages to a test statistic

Every number goes through the same five-step pipeline, so a price in Toronto and a price in New York are compared fairly.

  1. Collect

    Open the same product on the brand's official US and Canadian sites and record the list price, style number, source URL and date in collected_prices.csv. scrape.py automates this for pages that publish structured price data.

  2. Clean

    Parse price formats ($5,100 / C$ 6,600.00), drop sale prices and pages that redirect to the other country, and keep only products priced in both markets.

  3. Normalize

    Convert CAD to USD at the chosen rate, then add US sales tax and Canadian HST so both sides are what a shopper pays at the till.

  4. Test

    A paired t-test on log price ratios, the implied exchange rate in brand pricing, and an OLS regression of the gap on log US price, reproduced in Python with scipy.stats.

  5. Visualize

    Export one JSON file that this page reads and renders with hand-built SVG charts in plain JavaScript.

Limitations

Limitations & future work

What this analysis cannot tell you, and how I would extend it.

L1 · Sample size

A small, hand-picked sample

About ten models per brand cannot represent a full catalogue, and I chose well-known models rather than a random sample. Brand-level means rest on very few observations, so they are descriptive, not tests.

L2 · Coverage

Brands that could not be included

Some brands on my list publish no online prices (Audemars Piguet, Patek Philippe, Vacheron Constantin, A. Lange & Söhne, Panerai, Zenith, Piaget, Chanel bags). Others blocked automated access, failed to load, or sent the Canadian page to the US site (Louis Vuitton, Gucci, Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta, Balenciaga, Cartier, Loewe, Givenchy, Burberry), and Marc Jacobs disallows crawlers. Leaving them out may bias the result.

L3 · Price definition

Official list prices only

The data uses list prices before tax. It excludes sales, outlet prices, duty and tax refunds for travellers, and the waitlists and secondary-market premiums that decide what buyers really pay for a Rolex.

L4 · Freshness

A one-day snapshot

Prices were read once and are not refreshed by this page. Brands reprice when exchange rates move, so the gap measured here can change. A few prices were read from category listings rather than product pages; the CSV marks which.

L5 · Data source

Brand websites, not third-party sites

No single database lists official US and Canadian MSRP for every brand. I used each brand's own websites because they are the primary source. Third-party sites were checked and not used: Hudson's Bay has closed, Saks Canada blocks crawlers, Farfetch and NET-A-PORTER show retailer prices that can differ from MSRP, and Chrono24 is mostly resale listings.
